So, is maths real? 

I'm not certain the point of this book was to actually answer the question, and I certainly don't feel as if it did, but then again the book is aimed more towards those without the preconceptions about mathematics that a mathematics student would have. 

I think it was a interesting read, if not quite aimed towards somebody like me. I enjoyed Cheng's discussion on many of the ideas behind what maths is and how we've reached our current mathematical language. I didn't agree with all her points however, but it was a fun exercise working out why I disagreed with such points. 

Part of me wishes the text was *slightly* more technical, but that would make it harder for non-mathematics audiences to read, and I think trying to open up maths to more people is a great cause for a book.

In conclusion, 4/5, neat book, makes me want to read A Mathematician's Apology even more (I'll get around to it... one day, maybe.)
